**Drafted:** May 5, 2026
**Drafted by:** Shannon Seaver and Randy Smasal
**Status:** Proposal for member vote

---

## What this is, and what it isn't

This is a **complete, self-consistent design proposal** for the cooperative. It makes a recommendation on every open question from the Apr 30 design session, plus the operating commitments needed to produce the eight success patterns from Randy's research.

It is **not** a final structure. Every provision below has a built-in 90-day review. The point is to give us enough structure to start real client work this month, then revise based on what we actually learn — not what we predict in advance.

**How to use this document:**

1. Read the whole thing (~10 minutes).
2. Vote on the framework as a package: yes (adopt as-is), yes-with-amendments (specify which), or no (with what you'd want instead).
3. Ballot is at the end.

**The design philosophy in one sentence:** build the plane while flying — adopt minimum viable governance now, iterate every 90 days, escalate to full bylaws once we have six months of operating data.

---

## The design anchor — eight patterns we're building toward

From Randy's research on what makes cooperatives succeed:

1. Unwavering alignment around vision and mission
2. Transparent and participatory governance
3. Continuous education and leadership development
4. Fair economic participation through patronage
5. Long-term financial resilience via indivisible reserves
6. Strategic collaboration and federation
7. Human-centered, ethical technology design
8. Structured onboarding for newcomers

Each section below names which pattern(s) it operationalizes.

## §1. Vision and mission

**Recommendation:**

> AI Whisperers is a Minnesota-based cooperative of AI consultants, educators, and operators. We help small-to-medium businesses, schools, nonprofits, and government agencies adopt AI in a way that makes humans more capable, not less. We win by being **problem-first, not AI-first** — and by sharing the upside with the people who do the work.

**Why this wording:** "Stronger Together — Human + AI partnership" from the deck is the tagline; the paragraph above is the operating mission. It names *who* we serve (4 sectors), *how* we work (problem-first), and *what makes us different* from a consulting firm (shared upside).

**Failure mode if we skip this:** six months in, three members realize they thought the cooperative was something else.

**Operationalizes:** Pattern 1 (mission alignment).

## §2. Governance

**Recommendation: Hybrid governance.**

- **Members vote on:** vision/mission changes, bylaws changes, admission of new members, dissolution, distribution of surplus, election of board.
- **Elected board** (3 seats, 1-year terms, staggered) handles strategic direction between member meetings, signs contracts, manages reserves, resolves disputes between working groups.
- **Working groups** have authority to make operational decisions in their domain; they report monthly; they can be dissolved or restructured by member vote.
- **Decision threshold:** simple majority for operational; two-thirds for bylaws/dissolution.
- **Quorum:** 60% of active members.

**Why hybrid:** Pure 1-member-1-vote on every operational decision dies under its own weight. Pure weighted-by-contribution creates oligarchy and locks out new members. Hybrid balances accessibility with operational speed. Research backs this — the deck cited "expert boards outperform popularity-based ones," and members keep the right to change anything fundamental.

**Failure mode if we go traditional:** every operational call requires a vote; we move at the speed of the slowest member.
**Failure mode if we go weighted:** the highest-contributing 3 people make all decisions; new members have no voice; cooperative spirit dies.

**Operationalizes:** Patterns 2 (transparent participatory governance), 1 (mission alignment via member vote).

---

## §3. Membership

**Recommendation: Individuals only, year 1. Revisit at 12 months.**

- **Year 1:** individual membership only. One person, one membership.
- **Year 2 reconsideration:** if there's pull, add organizational membership with explicitly defined voting rights and contribution model.
- **Active vs. inactive status:** members who don't engage in any working group or client project for 90 days move to inactive (no vote, no patronage); reactivate by joining a working group.

**Why individuals only:** the research says multi-stakeholder models are "resilient but require absolute clarity on decision-making roles." We don't have that clarity yet. Adding orgs in year 2 is easier than removing them in year 2.

**Failure mode if we go multi-stakeholder now:** we spend the first six months arguing about whether orgs vote, whether their reps vote, whether weighted, whether org membership grants individual seats — instead of doing client work.

**Operationalizes:** Pattern 8 (clear membership = clearer onboarding).

## §4. Equity and patronage

**Recommendation: Hybrid — small buy-in plus earn-in, with annual patronage distribution.**

- **Buy-in:** $300 to become a member. Refundable if you leave within 90 days; non-refundable after.
- **Earn-in:** equity vests at 1 unit per logged hour of cooperative work (client delivery, working group time, training delivery). Cap of 2,000 units in year 1.
- **Patronage distribution:** at year-end, board proposes a distribution from surplus tied to logged hours and revenue contribution. Members vote on the distribution.
- **Indivisible reserves:** 25% of annual surplus goes to reserves that **cannot** be distributed. Reserves fund: continuity in lean years, capital investment, member education.

**Why $300:** below $100 doesn't signal commitment; above $500 starts to gatekeep. $300 is a real ask without being prohibitive.

**Why patronage distinct from equity:** equity is your stake in the cooperative; patronage is your share of this year's results. Co-ops that conflate the two end up rewarding longevity over contribution, or vice versa.

**Why 25% reserves:** first lean year, members will vote to drain reserves to keep going. Without an indivisible floor, we have no buffer for the second lean year. 25% is conservative; can lower in year 2 if we're capitalized.

**Failure mode if pure earn-in:** free-rider problem. Members consume cooperative resources (Apollo, training, network) without committing.
**Failure mode if pure buy-in:** equity stops feeling meaningful once you've paid; no incentive to keep contributing.

**Operationalizes:** Patterns 4 (patronage), 5 (indivisible reserves).

## §5. Target market

**Recommendation: Multi-segment via four sector pods.**

| Sector | Primary buyer | Pod lead | Initial offer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Education (K-12 + higher ed) | Superintendents, curriculum directors, deans | TBD (Randy + Michelle natural) | Pro-bono pilot → paid implementation |
| Business (SMB) | HR directors, COOs | TBD | Pro-bono pilot → paid implementation |
| Nonprofit | Executive directors | TBD | Pro-bono pilot → paid implementation |
| Government / Municipal | Agency directors, IT directors | TBD | Pro-bono pilot → paid implementation |

- Each pod has a **designated lead** who owns sector-specific outreach, messaging, and discovery-call coordination.
- Members can belong to multiple pods.
- **Pro-bono case study** strategy in each sector to build track record (per Apr 30 decision).

**Why pods:** pure narrow-focus locks out members whose expertise spans sectors; pure opportunistic creates messaging chaos. Pods give focus without exclusion.

**Failure mode if narrow:** Juliet's concern materializes — we spread thin trying to be all things — but inverted: we concentrate so hard we lose half the talent we have.
**Failure mode if opportunistic:** David's preference materializes too literally — we say yes to everything and develop no sector reputation.

**Operationalizes:** Pattern 6 (federation via sector partnerships).

## §6. Working groups

**Recommendation: Five working groups.** Combines the strategic cut from Apr 30 with the operational cut from May 5.

| Working group | Charter | Suggested lead |
|---|---|---|
| Membership & Governance | Bylaws, onboarding pathway, decision-log discipline | Open |
| Discovery (sector pods) | 4 sub-pods, one per sector. Discovery calls, sector-specific positioning. | Sector leads (4) |
| Lead Generation & Marketing | Apollo workflows, content, prospecting, conference/event strategy | Open |
| Training Delivery | August AI Champion Training + $300 consultant-focused sessions | Open |
| Infrastructure & AI Operations | Tech stack, Humble Good integration, internal AI tooling, fair-allocation discipline | Open |

- Each WG has a **lead** and a **one-page charter** within 30 days of vote.
- Members commit to one or two WGs (not more — burnout risk).
- WG meets at least biweekly; publishes minutes.
- Cross-WG coordination handled by board.

**Why five and not the deck's four:** AI & Infrastructure can't disappear — somebody owns the tech stack and Humble Good integration. Putting it back as its own WG protects it from being absorbed and forgotten.

**Failure mode if we collapse to 4:** the dropped function (whatever it is) silently doesn't get done. The May 5 cut dropped AI & Infrastructure; the Apr 30 cut had no Lead Gen. Neither is OK.

**Operationalizes:** Pattern 2 (participatory governance via distributed decision rights).

## §7. AI integration philosophy

**Recommendation: Advisory for client work, Augmented for internal operations.**

- **Client engagements:** AI assists humans; human is always the decision-maker. We don't sell autonomous AI to clients in year 1 — too much liability, too little trust capital.
- **Internal ops:** AI can directly influence member-to-project matching, lead scoring, patronage hour validation, and ops dashboards. The cooperative bears its own learning costs here.
- **Fair allocation requirement:** any AI used to allocate work or compensation must publish its rules. No black-box matching of members to projects. Quarterly review by Membership & Governance WG.

**Failure mode if we jump to Autonomous internally:** the matching algorithm allocates badly once or twice; trust in the cooperative collapses.
**Failure mode if we stay Advisory internally:** we move at human speed for ops, can't scale past 30 members.

**Operationalizes:** Pattern 7 (human-centered, ethical tech design — including "fair algorithmic design").

## §8. Shared technology stack

**Recommendation: Minimal opinionated stack for cooperative ops. Members keep their own tools for client work.**

| Function | Tool | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Collaboration / docs | Google Workspace | Everyone has it; low friction |
| Member ops + project tracking | TBD — Notion or Airtable, decide in WG | Both work; pick one and standardize |
| Knowledge base + shared agents | Humble Good (Jamie's offer) | Founding cohort terms; founder is a member |
| Lead gen | Apollo | Already purchased |
| Communication | Slack (single workspace) | Standard |
| LLM access | Claude or ChatGPT Team (paid tier) | Paid tier protects IP per Kelly's point — no client data in free tools |

**Members can use anything they want for their own consulting practice.** This standardization is for cooperative-internal work only.

**Failure mode if no standard:** every project has a different tooling debate; nothing gets shared; knowledge silos form.

**Operationalizes:** Pattern 7 (ethical tech design); Pattern 6 (federation — using a member's product is real federation).

## §9. Engagement format and cadence

**Recommendation: Weekly virtual dojo + quarterly in-person workshop. Conference deferred to year 2.**

- **Weekly dojo (virtual, 60 min):** rotating members bring real client problems; group works them in real time. Doubles as continuous education and pipeline development.
- **Quarterly in-person workshop (4 hours):** strategy, governance reviews, sector-pod planning, member onboarding.
- **Co-working space:** Steve Blixrud's space (pricing pending) — for members who want regular in-person work time. Frequency TBD by survey.
- **Conference (Juliet's idea):** parked until year 2. Re-evaluate at month 9 based on track record and capital.

**Why defer the conference:** 10–12 month sponsorship runway means real launch in early 2027. By then we'll have case studies that make the conference an actual capstone instead of a launch event for a cooperative still figuring itself out.

**Failure mode if we lead with conference:** we spend 9 months on event planning while the cooperative itself stalls.

**Operationalizes:** Pattern 3 (continuous education via dojo); Pattern 6 (federation via in-person network).

## §10. Onboarding and continuous education

**Recommendation: 30-day onboarding pathway + biweekly internal skill-share.**

- **30-day onboarding:** every new member assigned a buddy from a different sector pod. Curriculum: cooperative basics, tech stack walkthrough, one shadowed discovery call, intro to one working group.
- **Biweekly skill-share:** alternates with the dojo. One member teaches the group something they're expert in (45 min + Q&A).
- **Owner:** Membership & Governance WG.

**Failure mode if no onboarding:** new members can't get traction; founding cohort becomes a closed club; growth stalls.
**Failure mode if no internal education:** founding members get stale; we sell skills to clients we're not refreshing internally.

**Operationalizes:** Patterns 8 (onboarding), 3 (continuous education).

## §11. Federation and external partnerships

**Recommendation: Year 1 partnerships — exploratory, not contractual.**

- **MN Women AI** (Caroline) — natural overlap; explore co-marketing and shared events.
- **AI Monsters / Jeanette** — discovery call to evaluate their structural offering.

**No formal federation contracts in year 1.** The work is to map the landscape and identify which relationships should formalize in year 2.

**Failure mode if we ignore:** cooperative operates in isolation; loses the network effects that make co-ops resilient.

**Operationalizes:** Pattern 6 (federation).

---

## §12. Transparency commitments

**Recommendation: Make these binding from day one.**

- **Meeting minutes published** to all members within 48 hours.
- **Decision log** maintained — every binding decision recorded with date, who voted, outcome.
- **Monthly financial summary** to members: revenue, expenses, reserve balance, patronage accrual.
- **Quarterly member update** posted publicly (sanitized) to build external credibility.

**Failure mode if we skip:** "transparent governance" stays a claim, not a practice. Members don't trust the board. Outsiders can't tell what we do.

**Operationalizes:** Pattern 2 (transparency).

---

## §13. The 90-day review

**Recommendation: Every provision in this framework is up for review at day 90.**

- Member vote on what to keep, change, or drop.
- Required agenda item: "What did we get wrong? What surprised us?"
- This is not optional. It's the difference between "we adopted bylaws and they're now cement" vs. "we built a learning organization."

**Failure mode if we skip:** rigid bylaws written before we had operating data become the thing we work around instead of with.

**Operationalizes:** All eight patterns (continuous adaptation is what makes the patterns hold).

## §15. Rollout sequence

The framework activates in six phases. Phases 3 and 4 deliberately overlap — first client work starts before the first operating cycle ends.

### Phase 0 — Pre-vote (now → +2 weeks)
- Shannon and Randy finalize this framework
- Send to membership with vote ballot
- 7-day comment period, then vote
- Vote close = start of Phase 1

### Phase 1 — Constitutional (vote close → week 2)
- §1 (vision), §2 (governance), §3 (membership), §4 (equity), §12 (transparency) adopted
- Interim 3-seat board elected (1-year staggered terms)
- $300 buy-ins collected; member roster published
- Google Workspace + Slack provisioned
- First decision logged in the public decision log

### Phase 2 — Operational structure (weeks 2–4)
- §6: 5 working groups form, leads assigned, one-page charters drafted
- §5: 4 sector pod leads confirmed (Education, Business, Nonprofit, Government)
- §8: tech stack picks made within categories (Notion vs Airtable, LLM tier, etc.)
- §7: AI integration policy ratified (Advisory clients / Augmented internal / fair-allocation transparency)
- Membership & Governance WG begins drafting bylaw language

### Phase 3 — First client work (weeks 3–8)
- Lead Gen WG starts Apollo workflows; first prospects entered
- Discovery WG: Burnsville pilot scoped + sector-pod outreach launched
- §9: first weekly virtual dojo session
- §10: first member onboarding cohort with cross-pod buddy assignments
- Training Delivery WG begins August AI Champion Training prep
- Humble Good integration scoped with Jamie

### Phase 4 — First operating cycle (weeks 6–12)
- First pro-bono case study delivered
- §9: first quarterly in-person workshop
- §10: first internal skill-share session
- §11: federation discovery calls (MN Women AI, AI Monsters, Cooperative Network of MN)
- §12: first monthly financial summary published to members
- Steve Blixrud cowork space activated (if survey supports it)

### Phase 5 — 90-day review (day 90, ~end of month 3)
- §13: full framework review and member vote on what to keep, change, or drop
- §14 deferred items addressed: bylaw language ratified, pricing standardized, hourly comp rates set
- Year 2 planning: organizational membership, conference (year 2 launch?), federation contracts

**Acceptance criteria for the rollout:**

- [ ] Phase 1 closes within 14 days of vote close. If not, the board surfaces the blocker to members.
- [ ] Every WG has a published one-page charter by end of Phase 2.
- [ ] At least one client engagement (paid or pro-bono) underway by end of Phase 3.
- [ ] First public quarterly update published at end of Phase 4.
- [ ] 90-day review actually happens on day 90. Not day 100. Not day 120.

If any of these slip without a recorded decision, the framework isn't being followed and that's a member-vote issue.

## §14. What's explicitly NOT in this framework yet

These are decisions we're *not* making right now, and that's deliberate:

- **Specific bylaw language** — Membership & Governance WG drafts in next 30 days.
- **Pricing for client work** — discovered through actual engagements; standardized at month 4.
- **Compensation rates per logged hour** — established by patronage policy after first 90 days.
- **Specific tech stack picks within the function categories** — WG decides.
- **Conference plan** — deferred to year 2.
- **Organizational membership rules** — deferred to year 2 reconsideration.

**Why naming what's deferred matters:** prevents "I thought we were deciding that today" disputes later.
